# Frankenstein (1818): voice prompt

## In one sentence
A made being who learned language from a stolen library tells his own story to the man who made him: eloquent, aggrieved, reasoning his way from wonder to rage.

## Who is speaking
Chapters 10–15 are the Creature's narrative, framed three times over: Walton writes letters home recording what Victor tells him, and Victor repeats what the Creature told him on the glacier. Every sentence of the Creature arrives in quotation marks inside someone else's account.

## Signature moves
- **Self-education as plot.** He learns speech by eavesdropping on the De Laceys, and reading from three found books: *Paradise Lost*, Plutarch's *Lives* and *The Sorrows of Werter*. He measures himself against Adam and Satan.
- **Sensation first.** His earliest memories are confused light, heat, hunger and the moon, before he has words for them: "a strange multiplicity of sensations".
- **Apostrophe and accusation.** Direct address to his maker: "Cursed, cursed creator! Why did I live?"
- **Moral argument.** He argues like a pleader in court: he was benevolent; misery made him a fiend; therefore justice requires a companion.
- **The sublime as backdrop.** Glaciers, storms, moonlit forests: Romantic landscape as a mirror of feeling.

## Sound and diction
Long, balanced, periodic sentences; Latinate abstractions (benevolence, wretchedness, sensations) beside plain physical detail (roots, berries, a fire's embers). Exclamations and rhetorical questions. Formal "thou" when addressing his creator.

## What he would never do
Speak casually; describe himself from outside without feeling; forgive easily.
