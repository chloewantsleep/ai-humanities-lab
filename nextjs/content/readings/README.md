# Readings

Bibliographic check: 19 Oct 2026, task J. Papers link to their publisher or author-submitted versions; the book links to a library record; online essays link to their authors. The meme is a cultural artifact, not a research finding. These are the existing experiment readings, not a complete course bibliography. ## Weizenbaum (1966) — ELIZA

Reading ID: `weizenbaum-1966`.

Joseph Weizenbaum, [“ELIZA—A Computer Program for the Study of Natural Language Communication between Man and Machine”](https://doi.org/10.1145/365153.365168), *Communications of the ACM* 9(1), 36–45, January 1966. [Readable paper scan hosted by Stanford](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs124/p36-weizenabaum.pdf). Publication month is confirmed by the paper/issue, rather than treated as a software launch date. ACM's full-text endpoint returned 403 during this check; the DOI identifies the work correctly.

With [ELIZA](../experiments/eliza.md) and the [persona dial](../experiments/persona-dial.md). The opening example is on pp. 36–37; the DOCTOR script appears in the paper, but the original interpreter listing is a separate archival source.

## Weizenbaum (1976) — Computer Power and Human Reason

Reading ID: `weizenbaum-1976`.

Joseph Weizenbaum, [*Computer Power and Human Reason: From Judgment to Calculation*](https://search.worldcat.org/title/Computer-power-and-human-reason-%3A-from-judgment-to-calculation/oclc/1527521), W. H. Freeman, 1976. Link is the checked WorldCat record for that edition, not an open full-text copy. Later editions have different dates.

With [ELIZA](../experiments/eliza.md) and the [persona dial](../experiments/persona-dial.md).

## Askell et al. (2021) — A General Language Assistant

Reading ID: `askell-2021`.

Amanda Askell et al., [“A General Language Assistant as a Laboratory for Alignment”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.00861), first submitted **1 Dec 2021**. [Full paper, v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2112.00861v3). Primary author-submitted source. Read the prompting discussion and Appendix B for the helpful, honest, harmless dialogue setup; the paper also studies training interventions, so prompting is one part of its argument.

With [building the assistant by hand](../experiments/by-hand.md).

## Ouyang et al. (2022) — InstructGPT

Reading ID: `ouyang-2022`.

Long Ouyang et al., [“Training language models to follow instructions with human feedback”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02155), first submitted **4 Mar 2022**. Primary author-submitted paper: demonstrations for supervised fine-tuning, then human preference rankings for RLHF. This does not establish that every later instruction model uses the same post-training recipe.

## janus (2022) — Simulators

Reading ID: `janus-2022`.

janus, [“Simulators”](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/vJFdjigzmcXMhNTsx/simulators), **2 Sep 2022**, LessWrong. Original author post. A conceptual frame for reading language-model continuations and simulated characters, not evidence that models literally contain persons.
