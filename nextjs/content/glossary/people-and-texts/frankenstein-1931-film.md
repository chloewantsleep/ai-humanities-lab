# Frankenstein (1931 film)

**James Whale's film for Universal, with Boris Karloff as a monster who cannot speak: the image of the made being most people carry, and the opposite of Shelley's eloquent creature.**

## Why it matters here

The syllabus suggests watching it alongside the novel (September 15), and the contrast is the point. In the novel the creature's power is language: he argues, narrates, and quotes Milton. In the film he grunts. Popular images of AI have mostly followed the film (a powerful body without words), while the systems that arrived are the reverse: all words, no body. The course asks which version prepared us better.

## In the readings

Not a reading. Compare the novel's Volume II, chapter II, where the creature first speaks to Victor and asks to be heard ([Frankenstein](frankenstein.md)).
