# "ELIZA" (Weizenbaum, 1966)

**Weizenbaum's 1966 paper describing ELIZA, which explains exactly how the trick works and warns that the understanding people find in it is "the speaker's contribution."**

## Why it matters here

The paper is the blueprint for the [ELIZA](/eliza) experiment and the first statement of the course's theme. It opens with a program "unmasked": "once its inner workings are explained in language sufficiently plain to induce understanding, its magic crumbles away; it stands revealed as a mere collection of procedures." It names the program after Shaw's flower seller: "Like the Eliza of Pygmalion fame, it can be made to appear even more civilized, the relation of appearance to reality, however, remaining in the domain of the playwright." It separates the engine from the "script," which "is data; i.e., it is not part of the program itself." And it explains the choice of a therapist (see [the DOCTOR script](doctor-script.md)).

Its last point is pure Grice: a person talking to ELIZA defends "his sense of being heard and understood" by "attributing to his conversational partner all sorts of background knowledge, insights and reasoning ability. But again, these are the speaker's contribution to the conversation." The exchange it prints, beginning "Men are all alike. / IN WHAT WAY?", is the glossary's first running example.
