# Kenneth Colby

**Psychiatrist (1920–2001) at Stanford and later UCLA who proposed computer psychotherapy in 1966 and built PARRY, a program that simulated a paranoid patient.**

## Why it matters here

Colby is ELIZA's other parent and Weizenbaum's foil. Where Weizenbaum saw DOCTOR as a demonstration of how easily people are fooled, Colby and colleagues proposed, in 1966, that a program like it could actually do therapy, and Weizenbaum was appalled: in *Computer Power and Human Reason* he quotes their description of the therapist as "an information processor and decision maker with a set of decision rules" as one of the things that shocked him. Colby went on to build PARRY at the Stanford AI Lab, a model of a paranoid patient with internal states such as fear and anger, described in "Artificial Paranoia" (1971).

The two programs met. On 18 September 1972, PARRY (running at Stanford) and DOCTOR (at BBN) were connected over the ARPANET and talked to each other; Vint Cerf published the transcript as RFC 439, "PARRY Encounters the DOCTOR" (January 1973). It is the first chatbot-to-chatbot conversation on record, and a precursor of "let it write both sides."
