# Laughter (Bergson)

**Henri Bergson's 1900 essay on the comic: we laugh at "something mechanical encrusted on the living," the rigid habit where we expected a person to adapt.**

## Why it matters here

Recommended with *R.U.R.* on September 29, Bergson gives a precise name for the moment a chatbot stops seeming alive: the moment it repeats itself. The illusion holds while it adapts to you; it breaks, and often turns funny, when the same rule fires again, the same stock phrase comes back, or a base model loops on one sentence. His examples include a character who is "a regular talking-machine working automatically," and his account of imitation fits the experiments too: to imitate someone is to bring out the automatism in them.

Bergson also says laughter needs "an absence of feeling." Watching a chatbot fail, people laugh; talking to one that seems to understand them, they don't. The persona dial is a way of finding where that line falls.

- **Ch. I:** "To imitate any one is to bring out the element of automatism he has allowed to creep into his person" ([text](https://github.com/ll-26-27/eng-298ai-context/blob/24df8b8/_context/course-readings/course-readings-md/bergson-laughter.md?plain=1#L82)); "Something mechanical encrusted on the living" ([text](https://github.com/ll-26-27/eng-298ai-context/blob/24df8b8/_context/course-readings/course-readings-md/bergson-laughter.md?plain=1#L94)).
