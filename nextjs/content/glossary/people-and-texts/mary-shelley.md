# Mary Shelley

**English novelist (1797–1851) who wrote Frankenstein, the founding story of a made being that speaks and asks its maker for recognition.**

## Why it matters here

Hers is the first name on the syllabus and the first story most people reach for when they talk about AI, usually in the film's version (a mute monster) rather than hers (an eloquent one). The course reads the novel to recover the second: a creature whose first demand is to be listened to.

The novel's own origin is a writing game. In the summer of 1816, near Geneva, Shelley, Percy Shelley, and Lord Byron (with Byron's physician John Polidori) agreed to each write a ghost story; hers was the one that grew into a book. She tells it briefly in the 1818 preface and at length in her introduction to the revised edition of 1831. *Frankenstein* was first published anonymously, in three volumes, on 1 January 1818.

## In the readings

The 1818 preface: "I passed the summer of 1816 in the environs of Geneva. ... Two other friends ... and myself agreed to write each a story, founded on some supernatural occurrence" ([text](https://github.com/ll-26-27/eng-298ai-context/blob/24df8b8/_context/course-readings/course-readings-md/shelley-frankenstein-1818.md?plain=1#L41)), and "The following tale is the only one which has been completed" ([text](https://github.com/ll-26-27/eng-298ai-context/blob/24df8b8/_context/course-readings/course-readings-md/shelley-frankenstein-1818.md?plain=1#L43)).
