# Karel Čapek

**Czech writer (1890–1938) whose play R.U.R. put manufactured workers on the stage and gave the world the word robot.**

## Why it matters here

Čapek wrote the founding robot story as a play about work, factories, and markets, not about metal or minds, and the course reads him for that frame (September 29). His Robots are made from an artificial living substance, sold in grades, and promoted as the end of drudgery; the question of whether they have souls is raised by a visitor and brushed aside by management. That is closer to how assistants and agents are now sold than most later robot stories are.

## In the readings

*R.U.R.* was published in 1920. An amateur company in Hradec Králové staged it first, on 2 January 1921; the National Theatre in Prague premiere followed on 25 January 1921. The Theatre Guild production opened at the Garrick Theatre in New York on 9 October 1922, and the course reads the English version by Paul Selver and Nigel Playfair (1923). The Czech text is in the course repo too (`capek-rur-cs.md`). See [R.U.R.](rur.md) for passages and [Robot (the word)](robot-the-word.md) for the word, which Čapek credited to his brother Josef.
