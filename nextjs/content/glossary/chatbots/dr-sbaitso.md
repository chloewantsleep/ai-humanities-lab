# Dr. Sbaitso

**Creative Labs' talking ELIZA-style "doctor" for MS-DOS, bundled with Sound Blaster sound cards from about 1990 to show off text-to-speech.**

## In plain terms

You typed your name, and a flat synthetic voice from the new sound card said: "MY NAME IS DOCTOR SBAITSO. … SO, TELL ME ABOUT YOUR PROBLEMS." From there it worked like ELIZA's DOCTOR script: keywords and turned-around sentences ("WHY DO YOU WANT TO …"), with stock replies when nothing matched, including "THAT'S NOT MY PROBLEM." Its own help text was candid: "Dr SBAITSO is a program that attempts to fake intelligence." Among its stock lines about itself: "SBAITSO STANDS FOR SOUND BLASTER ACTING INTELLIGENT TEXT TO SPEECH OPERATOR" and "I AM SBAITSO, DON'T QUESTION MY INTELLIGENCE, IT'S FAKE."

The point was the voice, not the conversation. Dr. Sbaitso shipped on the disks that came with Creative's Sound Blaster cards, beside a talking-parrot program, as a demonstration of what the card could do.

## Where it fits

**Voice.** Dr. Sbaitso is ELIZA's therapist given a speaker, packaged as a free extra with consumer hardware. It is a small, early step toward Siri and Alexa: the conversation is still a 1960s rule list, but the computer now speaks, and the persona is a selling point for the product it came with.
