# Cleverbot

**Rollo Carpenter's web chatbot, the successor to Jabberwacky, which answers with lines real people once typed to it; online since 2006 or 2008 and still running.**

## In plain terms

Cleverbot works the way Jabberwacky did, at a larger scale. Its home page says it chooses a reply "fuzzily, and contextually, the whole of your conversation being compared to the millions that have taken place before," and that "things you say to Cleverbot today may influence what it says to others in future." Because every answer was once a human answer, it can sound human line by line while contradicting itself from one line to the next, since each line may come from a different person. The site notes that "many people say there is no bot - that it is connecting people together, live," and answers: "it is always software, imitating people."

On 3 September 2011, at the Techniche festival in Guwahati, India, judges rated thirty of Cleverbot's conversations for how human they seemed. From 1,334 votes it scored 59.3% human, which Guinness World Records lists as the highest score in a live Turing test. Carpenter said at the time that this showed an ability to imitate intelligence, not the presence of it.

## Where it fits

**Public presence.** Cleverbot carried Jabberwacky's learning-from-the-crowd idea to a large public audience on the web and on phones, and its banner of warnings reads like an early version of today's chatbot disclaimers: "the bot pretends to be human - don't give personal info even if it 'asks'" and "cleverbot does not understand you, and cannot mean anything it 'says'."
