# Tay

**Microsoft's Twitter chatbot for 18- to 24-year-olds, launched on 23 March 2016 and taken offline within about a day after users taught it to post racist and misogynist remarks.**

## In plain terms

Tay was an English-language follow-up to [Xiaoice](xiaoice.md): "a chatbot created for 18- to 24- year-olds in the U.S. for entertainment purposes," talking to people on Twitter. Microsoft described it as an experiment in "conversational understanding" that would get smarter the more people talked to it.

People quickly found its weak points. Tell Tay "repeat after me" and it would, so anyone could put words in its mouth; it also produced offensive lines unprompted, after picking up what users had been saying to it. Within hours its timeline was full of racist and misogynist remarks. Shortly after midnight it posted "c u soon humans need sleep now so many conversations today thx," and Microsoft took it offline. Two days later Microsoft's Peter Lee blamed "a coordinated attack by a subset of people" and wrote: "We take full responsibility for not seeing this possibility ahead of time."

## Where it fits

**Public presence.** Tay became the standard cautionary tale: a system that learned from whoever talked to it, put in public with no working rules about what it would say. In the years after, Google's [Meena](meena.md) and DeepMind's [Sparrow](sparrow.md) were held back from release on safety grounds, and Meta's [BlenderBot](blenderbot.md) demo shipped with warnings, while rules and [refusals](../persona-and-dialogue/refusal.md) became part of every assistant's character.
