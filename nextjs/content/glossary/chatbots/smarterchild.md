# SmarterChild

**ActiveBuddy's chatbot on AOL Instant Messenger and other messaging networks from June 2001: a "buddy" you could ask for weather and movie times, which most people used just to chat.**

## In plain terms

You added SmarterChild to your buddy list, and it sat there among your friends' screen names. It was built as an information service: type a question and get stock quotes, dictionary definitions, sports scores, or the weather. Every answer was written by people, a content team in New York (teachers, publishers, a rock musician) using a simple scripting language the engineers made for them. When it did not understand, it could turn your message into a question, as ELIZA did.

The company found that 97% of users were "simply chatting with SmarterChild for fun," a category it filed as "inane chat." A "profanity handler," written over a weekend, answered abuse with lines like "are all humans so rude?", and the bot became known for being snarky. Within its first year it reached 8 million unique screen names. ActiveBuddy struggled to make money from it (branded bots for magazines and films, customer-service bots), and Microsoft bought the company, by then called Colloquis, in October 2006 for its customer-service technology.

## Where it fits

**Public presence and platform.** SmarterChild put a chatbot where people already talked, in the messaging app, under a buddy name, with a personality. It showed that people would talk to a bot for company even when it was built to look things up. One of Siri's investors later cited it as evidence that there was consumer demand for chatbots.
