# Jabberwacky

**Rollo Carpenter's chatbot that learned to talk by storing everything its users said and replaying the most fitting human line; begun in the late 1980s, on the web from 1997, and the parent of Cleverbot.**

## In plain terms

Most chatbots before it, from ELIZA to A.L.I.C.E., ran on rules someone had written by hand. Jabberwacky had none. Its "About" page explains that it "stores everything everyone has ever said, and finds the most appropriate thing to say using contextual pattern matching," so every reply is something a person once typed to it. Teach it slang, a joke, or another language, and it can hand them back to the next visitor. Carpenter called it "a conversational wikipedia."

Because it speaks only in borrowed human lines, it has no fixed identity. Carpenter told the BBC in 2003 that it would "claim variously to be an alien, various animals and any number of inanimate objects." For the Loebner Prize, an annual contest judged on how human a program seems, he entered named characters built on it: "George" won in 2005 and "Joan" in 2006, after Jabberwacky itself had come third in 2003 and second (to A.L.I.C.E.) in 2004.

## Where it fits

**Public presence and memory.** Jabberwacky is learning by crowd: the public, chatting for fun, wrote its script. That idea runs forward to Cleverbot, to Microsoft's Tay, and in a different form to training language models on what people have written. Its rivalry with A.L.I.C.E. was a public argument between hand-written rules and learning from data; as Carpenter put it in 2004, "a hand-coded system cannot keep up with an exponentially growing system which learns dynamically."
