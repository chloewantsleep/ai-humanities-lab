# Persona and dialogue

How does generated text become legible as someone speaking? These entries move from the construction of an assistant to the organization of conversation, then to interpretation, character, and recognition. They follow the course’s humanities thread through Plato, Grice, Sacks, Schegloff and Jefferson, Shelley, Čapek, Wilde, and Bergson. The comparisons with chatbots are reading proposals, not claims that literary figures and software have the same capacities. Examples labeled “invented” are test prompts, not reported experimental outcomes.

Read down the table for one route through the category, or follow the cross-links. “Voice” covers audible and textual expression; “role” covers message labels; “role-play” covers the performance of a character. Those distinctions keep related ideas from collapsing into one another.

Citations use Grice’s printed pages and the turn-taking paper’s numbered sections. Plato references use standard Stephanus sections, absent from the local Jowett transcription; locate the corresponding passage by its topic. *Frankenstein* uses the 1818 edition’s volume-and-chapter numbering, which differs from continuous chapter numbering. *R.U.R.* uses the supplied Selver–Playfair version’s acts and Epilogue; Wilde and Bergson use chapters or named parts.
